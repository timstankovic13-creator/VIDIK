# Ottawa Case 003 — Paramedic Capacity Resource Allocation

## Decision boundary
**2023-12-06** — use the same historical decision boundary as Cases 001–002: Ottawa Council's 2024 budget approval date. Evidence published after the boundary is validation-only and cannot drive the historical recommendation.

## Decision question
Should Ottawa allocate a marginal municipal resource unit to additional paramedic capacity, and if so at what scale/deployment configuration, to improve emergency response while accounting for cost, capacity, effectiveness, uncertainty and opportunity cost?

## Candidate
`PARAMEDIC_CAPACITY_2024`

The candidate is deliberately defined as incremental paramedic capacity rather than the entire Ottawa Paramedic Service.

## Admissible evidence

### S01 — Ottawa Draft Budget 2023
The City of Ottawa's Draft Budget 2023 states that $1.8 million was allocated to hire 14 paramedic staff and procure associated vehicles to address response volume and population growth. This establishes a real pre-boundary resource/capacity planning observation. It does **not** establish that $1.8 million is a transferable marginal price for one additional paramedic, because the package includes associated vehicles and the budget is a program decision rather than a controlled marginal allocation experiment.

### S02 — Ottawa emergency-services operating context
The 2023 budget identifies emergency services as a core municipal service and explicitly links the additional paramedic staffing package to response volume and population growth. This establishes the intended capacity/activity pathway but not a candidate-specific causal effect on serious patient outcomes.

### S03 — Post-boundary operational validation
Later City reporting documents continuing response-time pressure, hospital offload delay and Level Zero events in 2023 and subsequent operational changes. These materials are retained for validation/context only when their publication is after the 2023-12-06 boundary; they are not executable historical inputs.

## Evidence-boundary rules
- No post-2023-12-06 source may enter the executable historical recommendation.
- No synthetic response-time, survival, avoided-death, utilization or ROI coefficient is permitted.
- The $1.8m/14-staff package must not be silently converted into a marginal causal production function.
- External emergency-medical-services evidence may inform transportability only when publication timing, population, intervention and counterfactual applicability are explicitly established.
- Program-level associations cannot be silently promoted to candidate-specific marginal effects.

## Marginal-chain audit

| Stage | Historical status | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Resource | Partial | A genuine $1.8m staffing/vehicle package for 14 paramedic staff is documented, but it is not an isolated marginal price for one additional deployable unit. |
| Capacity | Supported/partial | Fourteen additional staff plus vehicles establish planned capacity expansion, but deployment-hour/coverage conversion is not identified. |
| Activity | Supported | Additional paramedic capacity is intended to address response volume and population growth and therefore increase available emergency-response capacity. |
| Outcome | Missing for executable candidate | No admissible pre-boundary candidate-specific counterfactual estimate links this incremental package to a response-time or patient-level outcome. |
| System outcome | Missing | No admissible pre-boundary causal estimate links the candidate to avoided serious harm, mortality, disability or other validated system-level benefit. |

## Case 003 disposition
**NO RECOMMENDATION — BLOCKED.**

This is a successful fail-closed execution, not a model failure. VIDIK recognizes a real municipal investment and a plausible capacity pathway but refuses to manufacture a marginal outcome or serious-harm coefficient that the historical record does not support.

## Reopen conditions
A future executable recommendation requires, at minimum:
1. an admissible marginal resource definition separating staffing, vehicle and other package costs;
2. a defensible conversion from resources to deployable paramedic hours/coverage;
3. candidate-specific exposure/activity measurement, including geography, shift and call-demand context;
4. an outcome estimate with a defensible counterfactual for response time and/or patient outcomes; and
5. a serious-harm/system-outcome link with uncertainty, attribution and transportability explicitly recorded.

## Prospective learning plan
For a prospective capacity expansion, capture pre-deployment demand and response baselines, ambulance availability/Level Zero exposure, response-time distributions by priority, deployable hours, staffing/vehicle cost, geography and shift coverage, hospital offload delay, and patient-level serious outcomes where lawful and appropriate. Use a matched/comparison or other defensible causal design where feasible. Review at 6 months, 1 year, 2 years and 5 years; recalibrate only when observed outcomes remain causally attributable and the evidence chain remains intact.

## Validation-only context
Later Ottawa reporting describes substantial response-volume and hospital-offload pressures and Level Zero events, and later operational changes report reductions in Level Zero events. Those observations can inform the prospective outcome-learning layer, but they are deliberately **not** used to reconstruct a 2023 executable marginal recommendation.
