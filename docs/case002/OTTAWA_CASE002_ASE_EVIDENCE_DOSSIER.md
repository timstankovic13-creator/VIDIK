# Ottawa Case 002 — Automated Speed Enforcement Resource Allocation

## Decision boundary
**2023-12-06** — use the same historical decision boundary as Case 001: Ottawa Council's 2024 budget approval date. Evidence published after the boundary is validation-only and cannot drive the historical recommendation.

## Decision question
Should Ottawa allocate a marginal municipal resource unit to Automated Speed Enforcement (ASE), and if so where/at what scale, to maximize road-safety benefit while accounting for cost, capacity, effectiveness, uncertainty and opportunity cost?

## Candidate
`ASE_EXPANSION_2024`

The candidate is deliberately defined as an incremental ASE deployment rather than the entire road-safety program.

## Admissible evidence

### S01 — Ottawa Draft Budget 2023
Official City of Ottawa Draft Budget 2023. It states that the transportation investment included Automated Speed Enforcement camera installation at 17 locations, with the purpose of reinforcing speed limits and safety, and that ASE revenue was reinvested into Safer Roads Ottawa. This is planning/resource evidence, not a marginal cost-per-camera estimate.

### S02 — Ottawa ASE site-selection methodology
Official City of Ottawa ASE site-selection material states that school/park locations are selected using speed-limit compliance, high-end speeding, active-transportation exposure and collision history. It also documents Council's 2021 high-speed-road pilot and four planned pilot installations through 2024. This establishes candidate selection and planned activity, but not an incremental outcome estimate per marginal resource unit.

### S03 — Pre-boundary ASE program existence / operating context
Ottawa's ASE program was operating before the boundary, with the original eight-camera pilot launched in 2020. The City later reports the pilot's longitudinal speed outcomes, but those later analyses are retained only as validation evidence and are not used to reconstruct the 2023 decision.

## Evidence-boundary rules
- No post-2023-12-06 source may enter the executable historical recommendation.
- No synthetic dollar-per-camera, staffing, processing-capacity, collision-effect, or ROI number is permitted.
- External causal evidence may inform transportability only if publication timing and applicability are established.
- A program-level association cannot be silently converted into a candidate-specific marginal effect.

## Marginal-chain audit

| Stage | Historical status | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Resource | Partial | 2023 budget establishes ASE investment, but does not isolate an incremental CAD unit for one additional camera. |
| Capacity | Partial | Planned 17 installations establish physical deployment activity, but not marginal resource-to-capacity conversion. |
| Activity | Supported | ASE cameras enforce posted speed limits at selected sites. |
| Outcome | Validation-only | Later Ottawa analysis reports large improvements in speed compliance/high-end speeding, but it is post-boundary. |
| System outcome | Missing | No admissible pre-boundary candidate-specific estimate links one marginal ASE deployment to avoided serious injury/fatal collision. |

## Case 002 disposition
**NO RECOMMENDATION — BLOCKED.**

This is a successful fail-closed execution, not a model failure. The historical evidence ceiling prevents VIDIK from manufacturing a marginal cost/effect estimate from program-level planning evidence.

## Reopen conditions
A future executable recommendation requires, at minimum:
1. an admissible marginal resource definition (e.g. incremental CAD per camera or deployment package);
2. defensible conversion from resource to additional camera/site capacity;
3. candidate-specific exposure/activity measurement;
4. an outcome estimate with a defensible counterfactual/attribution design; and
5. a serious-harm/system-outcome link with uncertainty and transportability explicitly recorded.

## Prospective learning plan
If a real ASE deployment is authorized prospectively, capture the pre-installation site baseline, matched/comparison-site design, speed distribution, high-end speeding, collisions and serious-injury outcomes, implementation cost, enforcement-processing workload and site exposure. Review at 6 months, 1 year, 2 years and 5 years; recalibrate only when observed outcomes are admissible and the causal chain remains intact.

## Validation-only context
Ottawa subsequently reported that at the original eight pilot sites posted-speed-limit compliance rose from 16% before implementation to 57% within three months, 69% after one year and 81% after three years; high-end speeding fell from 14% to 4%, 2% and 0.7%, respectively. These figures are deliberately **not** used as blind 2023 inputs. The later City material also reports that the ASE program generated revenue that funded road-safety initiatives. Those later observations are reserved for outcome-learning/validation layers.
