# Case 001 — Real Marginal Reconstruction Audit

**Branch:** `dev-case001-real-marginal-reconstruction`  
**Decision boundary:** 2023-12-06  
**Purpose:** Apply the merged marginal-resource reconstruction layer to the actual pre-boundary Ottawa evidence without inventing a recommendation.

## Result

**NO DEFENSIBLE COMPLETE MARGINAL CHAIN FOUND.**

VIDIK remains **BLOCKED** for allocation. The evidence supports several real component facts, but it does not support a causally identified, Ottawa-specific marginal chain of:

**CAD resource → additional capacity → additional activity/placements → expected outcome → serious-harm/system impact.**

That is a successful fail-closed result.

## Evidence actually available at the boundary

### 1. Municipal resource/capacity context

Ottawa's Draft Budget 2024, tabled before the boundary, reported approximately 17,000 community-housing units, 4,700 households receiving housing benefits, 29 organizations supporting homeless and unsheltered people, and 1,697 affordable units in development/construction (504 under construction and 1,193 in pre-development). It is admissible as pre-decision municipal context, not as a marginal causal model. Source: City of Ottawa, Draft Budget 2024.

### 2. A real capital project ratio exists, but it is not a marginal Housing First effect

Ottawa's Draft Budget 2023 reported a Shepherds of Good Hope project with **48 supportive housing units** as part of a **$7.8 million multi-year investment**. The same document also reported other projects with different funding/unit relationships. This demonstrates that real project-level CAD-to-unit observations existed, but they are heterogeneous capital projects and do not establish a constant or marginal CAD-to-additional-unit production function for Housing First at the 2023-12-06 boundary.

A purely arithmetic normalization would be:

- $7.8M / 48 units = **$162,500 per listed unit**
- $1M / $162,500 = **6.15 listed units per $1M**

VIDIK **does not promote 6.15 units/$1M into a marginal parameter**. Doing so would assume linearity, divisibility, identical project mix, identical financing/leverage, land availability, construction timing, and candidate equivalence—all unsupported.

### 3. Housing First causal outcome evidence exists

The At Home/Chez Soi randomized evidence provides a causal benchmark: approximately **42 percentage points adjusted difference** in stable housing at one year between Housing First and treatment as usual in the studied high-need population (95% CI 36–48). This is admissible external causal evidence.

It is **not** an Ottawa-specific marginal effect. It cannot be multiplied by the 48-unit project or the 6.15-unit arithmetic ratio without a supported bridge from Ottawa marginal resources to additional Housing First placements and from those placements to the studied population/implementation conditions.

### 4. Serious-harm pathway is not identified

The admissible evidence set does not establish a candidate-specific estimate of serious harm prevented per additional marginal Housing First placement, nor a validated Ottawa causal bridge from stable housing to the required serious-harm endpoint for this allocation problem.

Therefore the final chain cannot be completed even if the capacity and housing-stability components are considered separately.

## Gate-by-gate reconstruction

| Chain element | Real evidence found | VIDIK status | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| CAD resource | Municipal budget/resource observations | Observed/contextual | Does not identify a marginal allocation amount/effect |
| Additional capacity | 48-unit supportive project; other project-level unit counts | Observed/project-level | Not a marginal, candidate-specific production function |
| Activity/placements | Housing First service activity exists historically | Observed/contextual | No admissible CAD→additional placements conversion |
| Expected housing outcome | RCT stable-housing effect | Observed causal external evidence | Transportability/marginal bridge to Ottawa is not established |
| Serious-harm/system impact | No complete pre-boundary candidate-specific marginal estimate | Missing | No defensible causal endpoint mapping |

## Why this is not a recommendation

The tempting calculation would be to combine a project capital ratio with the Housing First RCT effect and call the result a $1M marginal impact. That would manufacture at least two unsupported bridges:

1. **project-average capital cost → marginal Ottawa Housing First capacity**; and
2. **additional Ottawa capacity → Ottawa serious-harm reduction**.

The reconstruction layer is specifically designed to reject that move.

## Decision

**Case 001 remains blocked.**

No real candidate-specific marginal chain is sufficiently evidenced to populate the recommendation engine. The next evidence-development target is therefore not optimization; it is finding admissible pre-2023-12-06 evidence that can identify a genuine marginal resource-to-capacity relationship and an independently identified outcome/serious-harm bridge.
