# Case 001 — Real Ottawa Marginal Chain Audit

**Decision boundary:** 2023-12-06

## Finding

The search for a pre-boundary incremental resource → incremental capacity chain was successful **only for the resource/capacity portion**. It did **not** produce an admissible outcome comparison attributable to that increment. The full marginal allocation chain therefore remains blocked.

### Pre-boundary resource → capacity evidence

The strongest admissible example found is Ottawa's September 6, 2023 affordable-housing capital decision. Council approved up to **$18,553,914 of Rapid Housing Initiative Round 3 funding for 54 supportive-housing units at 56 Capilano Drive**. The same decision also records up to $24.15M in provincial funding for 138 units at Mikinak and other project-specific allocations. This establishes a real pre-boundary resource-to-planned-capacity observation. citeturn0search5turn0search7

Ontario also reported on June 16, 2023 that **more than $8.3M** through the Social Services Relief Fund would create **57 supportive-housing units** in Ottawa, providing an independent pre-boundary resource-to-capacity observation. citeturn0search0

The City’s 2023 Draft Budget, adopted March 1, 2023, also documents **$15M in new 2023 funding** supporting more than 1,000 affordable/supportive units across named projects, including a $7.8M multi-year investment associated with 48 supportive units on Murray Street. citeturn0search3turn0search38

### Outcome evidence found — but not linked causally to the increment

A historical City Council record from February 2019 reports that Ottawa's Housing First program had housed 882 participants since April 2015 with a **92% housing-retention success rate**, while also noting more than $26M in housing/homelessness agency funding in 2019. This is useful outcome evidence, but it does not identify an incremental resource change, a counterfactual, or an outcome difference attributable to that change. citeturn1search1

The City's 2023 Housing and Homelessness Progress Report reports **$39,390 average annual operating funding per supportive-housing unit**, 78% retained for 1+ year and 56% for 2+ years. However, that report is an annual 2023 outcome report and its publication timing is not established as before the 2023-12-06 boundary. It is therefore **post-boundary validation evidence, not admissible blind evidence for Case 001**. citeturn1search10

## What can be calculated — descriptively only

The post-boundary $39,390 figure can be used as a validation/audit reference, not as an admissible historical marginal parameter:

**$1,000,000 / $39,390 = 25.39 units-equivalent of annual operating funding**.

Applying the reported 78% one-year retention descriptively gives:

**25.39 × 0.78 = 19.80 unit-years retained for 1+ year**.

These calculations are diagnostic only and are **not admitted as a marginal production function**.

## Why the chain still fails the marginal gate

1. The strongest pre-boundary resource/capacity observations are project-level allocations and planned unit counts, not an identified marginal cost of the next unit.
2. No admissible pre-boundary source found in this search identifies an outcome comparison attributable to the incremental resource/capacity change.
3. Historical retention figures do not establish the counterfactual outcome that would have occurred without the increment.
4. The evidence does not establish that another $1M would purchase a fixed number of additional units under the same conditions.
5. No admissible Ottawa-specific causal estimate connects an incremental supported placement to a serious-harm/system outcome.
6. Therefore the chain cannot honestly be promoted to `CAD -> marginal capacity -> activity -> outcome -> serious-harm impact`.

## Classification

| Layer | Status | Reason |

| Marginal causal effect | **missing** | no admissible incremental outcome comparison |
| Serious-harm/system effect | **missing** | no admissible Ottawa-specific bridge |
| Full marginal chain | **BLOCKED** | fail closed |

## Evidence ceiling

**Search conclusion:** the pre-2023-12-06 record can establish real resource commitments and associated planned capacity, and it contains historical outcome observations. It does **not** establish the causal link required to turn those observations into a marginal allocation function.

The evidence ceiling at the Case 001 boundary is therefore:

**resource change → planned capacity: supported**

**resource change → incremental outcome: not identified**

**incremental outcome → serious-harm/system effect: not identified**

No recommendation should be manufactured to bridge those gaps.

## Decision

**Case 001 remains `NO RECOMMENDATION — BLOCKED`.**

The development reconstruction layer should preserve the real pre-boundary resource/capacity evidence and the separate historical outcome evidence, while explicitly refusing to infer the missing causal links.

## Next step

The next useful work is not another numerical derivation from the same evidence. It is either:

1. obtain a genuinely pre-boundary evaluation/contract/program dataset that measures an incremental resource change against an outcome comparison; or
2. formally close the historical reconstruction as **evidence-insufficient at the 2023-12-06 boundary** and move to a forward-looking evidence-acquisition design where the missing marginal quantities can be measured prospectively.
