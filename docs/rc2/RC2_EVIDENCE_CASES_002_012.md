## Evidence notes

### Case 002 — Automated Speed Enforcement
Ottawa's 2023 budget identified automated speed enforcement camera installation at 17 locations, and the adopted budget contains a dedicated ASE camera capital line. The 2023 Road Safety Action Plan also specified 13 new cameras plus four pilot locations. This establishes a concrete historical resource/activity chain. citeturn0search0turn0search23turn0search11

Later Ottawa reporting provides strong behavioural outcome evidence: at original pilot locations, posted-speed compliance increased substantially over time and high-end speeding declined. That evidence is valuable for current learning, but it does not by itself establish a candidate-specific causal effect on collisions/serious harm or the marginal benefit of one additional camera/resource unit. Therefore the historical recommendation remains blocked.

### Case 003 — Paramedic capacity
The 2023 draft budget explicitly proposed $1.8M for 14 paramedic staff and associated vehicles to address response volume and population growth. This gives a clean historical resource/capacity exposure. citeturn0search0

Later reporting provides system-level outcome evidence, including large reductions in Level Zero exposure. That is useful for outcome learning, but a recommendation-grade chain still requires marginal exposure, appropriate counterfactual, attribution, and a serious-harm pathway. Remains blocked.

### Cases 004–005
The 2023 budget establishes 29 additional OPS personnel, but that aggregate staffing investment is not enough to identify the marginal effect of the specific frontline staffing candidate. OC Transpo Special Constable evidence likewise requires a candidate-specific resource → activity → outcome chain before promotion.

## RC2 conclusion

**No case in 002–012 is promoted to a recommendation on this pass.** This is deliberate. The evidence layer is now materially richer, but the remaining gaps are causal attribution, candidate-specific marginal exposure, counterfactual definition, transportability, and serious-harm pathways. Those gaps must be resolved before VIDIK is allowed to produce a substantive historical recommendation.

Next executable work:
1. acquire candidate-specific quantitative evidence for the strongest candidates;
2. construct explicit counterfactuals and marginal-unit definitions;
3. complete transportability/staleness/conflict analysis;
4. run the blind engine only on evidence that clears those gates;
5. adversarially test any resulting recommendation before promotion.
