# VIDIK RC2 Decision Comparison + Adversarial Stress Protocol

## Common marginal resource unit

No cross-candidate recommendation comparison is permitted until each candidate is expressed in the same marginal resource unit for the decision being asked. Accepted units include:

- incremental dollars;
- incremental FTEs;
- officer/paramedic/firefighter hours;
- equivalent capacity units when the production relationship is explicitly documented.

A candidate cannot win by being assigned a different resource unit or an incomparable outcome denominator.

## Required comparison record

For every model-eligible candidate:

`candidate -> marginal unit -> incremental capacity -> incremental activity -> outcome effect -> system effect -> serious-harm effect -> cost -> uncertainty -> equity -> opportunity cost`

The status quo is always included as a candidate baseline.

## Blind evaluation

The expanded evidence set is passed to VIDIK without revealing a desired winner. The run must record:

- eligible vs blocked status;
- expected marginal benefit;
- uncertainty interval/distribution;
- recommendation-flip thresholds;
- Why and Why-not rationale;
- status-quo incremental effect;
- alternatives and opportunity cost;
- evidence ceiling and provenance lineage.

## Adversarial suite

For every candidate that reaches model evaluation, rerun after:

1. removing the strongest evidence item;
2. widening the largest uncertain parameter range;
3. changing plausible correlation coefficients;
4. reversing or weakening a conflicting evidence direction;
5. lowering transportability below threshold;
6. applying a staleness penalty;
7. changing normative priorities within the allowed policy range;
8. forcing the status quo as the counterfactual;
9. adding the strongest plausible alternative;
10. testing EVPI/EVSI/VOI for the evidence gaps.

The result must identify whether the recommendation is stable, what flips it, and whether the evidence required to resolve the flip has positive value of information.

## Promotion rule

A recommendation that flips under a plausible uncertainty/evidence scenario is not treated as robust. It may still be shown as a conditional result, but the default decision must expose the instability and may remain `NO RECOMMENDATION` when the evidence cannot support a defensible choice.
